Today · the flow

Stance: warm, sober, unhurried

You do not have a slot figured out and you do not need to pretend otherwise. The honest posture is that you are listening for where she resonates.

Target roughly 60 percent her, 40 percent you. Resist filling silences. The information you need is in what she volunteers, not in what she confirms.

Say nothing today that you would not be willing to repeat in writing tomorrow.

1. Open and hand it over

Two minutes of thanks, then give her the floor and leave it with her.

"Before I say anything about how things work here, I would rather hear where you actually are. What are you weighing at the moment?"

Then stop talking. What she raises first, unprompted, is the most reliable signal you will get all call.

2. Listen for resonance, do not assign

You are not matching her to a project today. You are finding out which problems make her lean forward.

  • Which of your areas does she ask a second question about?
  • Does she talk about people, or about methods?
  • When she describes the humanitarian allocation idea, is it hers or is it a reflection of yours?

3. Four probes, spaced out

  • Your thesis makes a claim someone could disagree with. What is it?
  • What broke when you took the model to a situation it was not built for?
  • If you had five years on one problem rather than variety, what would you want it to be?
  • What would you push back on in how I have described this work?

The fourth is the one to protect. You have never seen her disagree with you, and a student who only agrees is hard to advise.

4. How work begins here

Say it plainly, as you did with the other candidate. Students arrive with methods, judgment, and earned context. The problems come from partner organisations and from where the initiative is already pointed.

Include the unglamorous part: partner meetings, data that does not arrive clean, months before a model exists, likely contributing to someone else's project before owning one.

5. The funding conversation

You told her US doctorates are funded positions. That is true and you are not walking it back. What you are adding is the texture underneath it.

"Admitted doctoral students here are supported, with tuition covered and a stipend. That part is real. What I can personally commit to is the first year. Beyond that we build the runway together, through grants and fellowships, and learning to write those is part of the training rather than a chore attached to it. Nobody has unlimited money, including me."

6. The offer, framed as joint

What you can genuinely offer today, without committing the department or promising admission:

  • To advise her fellowship applications, starting now
  • To support the first year if she is admitted
  • Continuation as a joint endeavour, not a probation

Avoid the word performance. To someone who has not yet applied it reads as a threat. "We build it together" carries the same expectation and does not.

Correcting the publication idea, warmly

"A thesis being findable is not the same as being published. Publication means a community that works on your problem has looked at your argument and decided it holds. A thesis reports what you did. A paper has to argue why anyone should care. That difference is most of what a doctorate teaches."

Say it once, lightly, and move on. It is easier out loud than in writing, which is why it waited for today.

Why the one-year frame is not a retreat

"Fully funded" describes the norm and the department's intent. It does not mean a five-year endowment attached to each student. In practice support is assembled year to year from teaching lines, grant-funded research lines, and fellowships, and every advisor's reach is bounded by their awards.

So the accurate statement is not a smaller version of what you told her. It is the texture underneath the same claim, and saying it plainly is the thing that will make her trust the rest.

Watch for: if she hears it as a downgrade, say so directly and explain the mechanics. Do not let it sit as a vague worry.
