SOMEONE HAS TO CHOOSE:

  • Where the mobile kitchen visits first
  • Which immigrants go to which city
  • Who gets the last shelter bed
  • Who receives the kidney

Who will make these connections?

None of these allocations were based on money. Someone needed to decide how to choose among qualified possibilities, and these decisions are the mathematics and the algorithms of this course.

Course description

Mathematical modeling and algorithms for decision problems in public and nonprofit systems that serve people, including shelter, food security, refugee resettlement, child welfare, and nonprofit operations. Topics include modeling and algorithms that allocate scarce resources for humans without a price mechanism, problem scoping and co-design, representing tradeoffs among those served, decisions that withstand sparse and uncertain data, explaining and defending recommendations, decision support and interactivity, and responsible deployment.

Who should enroll

Anyone comfortable with quantitative reasoning is welcome. Optimization, algorithms, statistics, machine learning and microeconomics are all good entry points, and the opening weeks will cover core concepts so that folks arriving from different perspectives can follow the rest of the course. Some programming experience is useful. If you are unsure whether your background fits, write me an email and we can discuss together.

The project

The course has a semester-long project concerning real allocation, starting from scoping and ending with recommendations that you can defend. More details will come with the syllabus.
